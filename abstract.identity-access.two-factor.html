Two-step verification

Off

After your password, you enter a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone. Your account stays protected even if someone learns your password.

  • You need an authenticator app

    Any app that shows 6-digit codes works, such as Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator.

  • You're asked at every sign-in

    There is no way to skip the code on a device you use often.

  • It stays on

    You can't turn it off yourself. If you lose your phone and your backup codes, someone else at Abstract who manages users can reset it.

    Two-step verification

    On

    Each time you sign in, you enter a 6-digit code from your authenticator app after your password.

    Your new backup codes

    The codes you had before no longer work.

    This is the only time these codes are shown. Keep them somewhere safe and away from your phone.

    Backup codes

    A backup code signs you in when you can't use your authenticator app. Each works once. They can't be shown again, so if you've lost them or used most of them, create a new set.

    New phone, or lost your phone and your backup codes?

    Someone else at Abstract who manages users can reset two-step verification. A reset signs you out everywhere; you then set it up again if it is required, or whenever you choose. You can't turn it off or reset it yourself.
